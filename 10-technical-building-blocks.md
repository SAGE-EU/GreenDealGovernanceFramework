The Technical Framework defines the common capabilities required to establish an interoperable, trusted, sovereignty-preserving, and scalable Green Deal Data Space (GDDS). It provides guidance on the standards, specifications, and reference architectures that support secure, decentralised data sharing, while allowing participants the flexibility to adopt distributed yet interoperable technologies that best fit their existing infrastructures and domain-specific requirements.

Technical capabilities form the foundation for operating and scaling the Green Deal Data Space (GDDS). Their purpose is to ensure secure, trusted, interoperable, and value-driven data exchange between diverse participants across sectors and Member States.&#x20;

The objective of these technical areas is to:&#x20;

* Identify essential capabilities required for GDDS implementation.&#x20;
* Provide clear references to standards and specifications that can be adopted or adapted by participants.&#x20;
* Enable technical interoperability through shared approaches, reducing integration costs and ensuring that different systems, platforms, and tools can work together seamlessly.&#x20;
* Enable AI-ready, FAIR, and interoperable data assets that support analytics, automation, and innovation.

The GDDS draws on widely recognised open standards, specifications, and reference architectures. Leveraging these allows participants to make informed technical choices while ensuring compatibility across the ecosystem. This also promotes reusability of solutions, easier onboarding, and faster scaling.&#x20;

The GDDS adopts a federated architecture in which participants retain control over their data while enabling secure and interoperable exchange across organisational and national boundaries.

Technical capabilities are grouped into three interconnected areas:&#x20;

1. Data Interoperability&#x20;

* Common semantic data models, metadata standards, data formats, APIs, and data exchange protocols to enable interoperable and consistent data exchange across the GDDS.
* Metadata management capabilities supporting resource discovery, quality assessment, FAIRness, versioning, and lifecycle management.
* Provenance and traceability mechanisms to capture the origin, lineage, and history of shared data and related assets, enabling verification of their integrity.

2. Data Sovereignty and Trust&#x20;

* Policy definition, negotiation, and enforcement mechanisms governing data access, sharing, and usage. (Data Sovereignty)
* Usage control mechanisms enabling data providers to retain control over how their data is accessed, processed, and reused. (Data Sovereignty)
* Digital identities, verifiable credentials, and trust services for participants, services, and digital assets. (Trust)
* Mechanisms for authentication, authorisation, and establishment of trusted interactions between participants. (Trust)

3. Data Value Creation Enablers&#x20;

* Tools and services for data/service discovery, matchmaking, and registration.&#x20;
* Capabilities supporting data processing, AI services, federated analytics, digital twins, and other value-added applications that enable innovation within the GDDS.
* Capabilities supporting automated participant onboarding, service registration, and lifecycle management.

By aligning these technical capabilities with the governance, legal, and operational layers, GDDS will enable a federated ecosystem in which participants can securely discover, share, access, and create value from data while preserving sovereignty, ensuring regulatory compliance, and supporting innovation across environmental domains
