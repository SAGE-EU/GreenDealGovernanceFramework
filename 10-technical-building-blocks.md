The Technical Framework defines the common capabilities required to establish an interoperable, trusted, sovereignty-preserving, and scalable Green Deal Data Space (GDDS). It provides guidance on the standards, specifications, and reference architectures that support secure, decentralised data sharing, while allowing participants the flexibility to adopt distributed yet interoperable technologies that best fit their existing infrastructures and domain-specific requirements. This section refers to the D2.1 and D3.1 documentation that covers the GDDS Architecture and Implementation, and they are published as first version reports by the SAGE Consortium (see [GDDS Architecture & Implementationfirst-version).](gdds-infrastructure-architecture.md)

Technical capabilities form the foundation for operating and scaling the Green Deal Data Space (GDDS). Their purpose is to ensure secure, trusted, interoperable, and value-driven data exchange between diverse participants across sectors and Member States.&#x20;

The objectives of these technical areas are to:&#x20;

* Identify core capabilities required for GDDS implementation.&#x20;
* Provide clear references to standards and specifications that can be adopted or adapted by participants.&#x20;
* Enable technical interoperability through shared approaches, reducing integration costs and ensuring that different systems, platforms, and tools can work together seamlessly.&#x20;
* Encourage inclusion of AI-ready, FAIR, and interoperable data assets in the SAGE Data Space that support analytics, automation, and innovation.

The GDDS draws on widely recognised open standards, specifications, and reference architectures. Leveraging these allows participants to make informed technical choices while ensuring compatibility across the ecosystem. This also promotes reusability of solutions, easier onboarding, and faster scaling.&#x20;

The GDDS adopts a distributed architecture in which participants retain control over their data while enabling secure and interoperable exchange across organisational and national boundaries.

Technical capabilities are grouped into three interconnected areas:&#x20;

1. **Data Interoperability:** common semantic data models, metadata standards, formats, APIs, and exchange protocols (Data Models); the patterns and interfaces for moving data between participants (Data Exchange); and the mechanisms that capture origin, lineage, and downstream use (Provenance and Traceability).
2. **Data Sovereignty and Trust:** how participants are identified, authenticated, and assured, and how their attestations are issued and verified (Identity and Attestation Management); how verified participants and services are trusted across organisational boundaries and how authorisation is represented and conveyed (Trust Framework); and how agreed access and usage conditions are expressed as machine-readable policies and enforced at runtime (Access and Usage Policies Enforcement).
3. **Data Value Creation Enablers:** how data, services, and offerings are described in a standardised, machine-readable way (Data, Services, and Offerings Descriptions); how they are published and made findable (Publication and Discovery); and the value-added services built on discovered data (Value Creation Services).

By aligning these technical capabilities with the governance, legal, and operational layers, GDDS will enable a federated ecosystem in which participants can securely discover, share, access, and create value from data while preserving sovereignty, ensuring regulatory compliance, and supporting innovation across environmental domains
