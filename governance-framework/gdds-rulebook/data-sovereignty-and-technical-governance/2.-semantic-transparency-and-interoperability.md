# 2. Semantic transparency and interoperability

## Rule&#x20;

Participants are required to declare the data models, vocabularies, code lists, or classifications used in their data offerings, together with references to their authoritative definitions. According to the GDDS Architecture, this is foreseen to be collected in Registries such as the Data Model Registry, Vocabulary Registry, etc.&#x20;

This obligation of semantic transparency enables other participants, intermediaries, and value-added service providers to assess semantic compatibility and build cross-domain applications without ambiguity.&#x20;

In the same way, and where applicable to the offering, participants are required to declare how the objects the data describes are identified and related: the identifier scheme or schemes used for its principal object types (for example products, parties, locations, assets, logistic units, and the datasets or resources themselves), the identifier syntax and the authoritative registry or issuing authority for each, the link types expressing relationships between identified objects, and, where the offering conveys events about objects, the event model.

Recognised standards families are referenced rather than imposed and are treated as examples at an equal level of detail. For product- and supply-chain-centric interoperability, the GS1 family provides identification of products, parties, locations, and logistic units, web resolution, and a supply-chain event model; for other domains, schemes such as INSPIRE and EPSG identifiers apply to geospatial resources, and the LEI to legal entities. The choice of family is a domain decision, made transparent through the declaration required above.&#x20;

{% hint style="warning" %}
_Note: The mechanism by which these identifier declarations are collected, including whether a dedicated identifier-scheme registry is used, is to be confirmed with WP5._
{% endhint %}

### Procedure&#x20;

GDDS does not impose a single, uniform data model across all domains. Instead, it adopts a federated and layered semantic approach that encourages reuse of existing, recognised standards where mature and adopted, while permitting participants to use domain-specific or local models where no widely adopted standard exists, provided these are clearly documented, versioned, and referenced in the metadata of the published data product.  &#x20;

The GDDS supports an incremental model of semantic integration, reducing initial barriers to entry while enabling participants to raise their semantic interoperability maturity over time. This reflects the diversity of the initial 10 GDDS use cases and avoids creating disproportionate barriers to participation.&#x20;

## Responsible body&#x20;

Each participant is responsible for declaring and documenting the semantics of its own offerings. The DSGA should maintain oversight of accepted data models, vocabularies, and identifier schemes to ensure cross-domain alignment where necessary. The supported levels of semantic abstraction and the technical specifications are described in the Technical Framework (see [Data Interoperability — Data Models)](../../../11-data-interoperability/data-models.md).&#x20;
