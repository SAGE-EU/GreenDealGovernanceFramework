# GDDS Governance Authority & Model

From the outset, the Governance Framework is treated as an evolving framework, designed to mature alongside the data space itself. Rather than prescribing a single, fixed governance structure from the beginning, the GDDS Governance Model intentionally distinguishes between:&#x20;

* Project-phase governance (MVP1), focused on design, coordination, enablement, and piloting, and&#x20;
* Operational governance (MVP2) that is intended for a fully running data space with active data exchange and long-term sustainability.&#x20;

MVP1 will be set up in late 2026 to allow the SAGE Project to use this preliminary structure for practical data space governance, while enabling experimentation and incremental refinement as the data space itself matures, and as real-world needs are addressed.  More significant adjustments in the MVP1 Governance Playbook would be deferred until later in the project, when a full MVP2 structure will be finalised and established, so that it is up and running for use once SAGE transitions from a "project" to a sustainable production data space.

Fundamental governance principles were explored during initial workshops and research activities focused on identifying relevant governance layers, roles, and responsibilities. This preliminary exploration emphasised the need for:&#x20;

* multi-layer and decentralised governance,&#x20;
* separation of governance and operational activities,&#x20;
* trust and compliance as core governance concerns, &#x20;
* external regulatory compliance and internal compliance with rules and technical requirements,&#x20;
* and alignment with the data space principles and standards such as the ones defined in DSSC.&#x20;

Building on these foundations, co-creation sessions with SAGE consortium participants progressively refined the governance model, incorporating feedback on first simplicity, then tailored federation, subsidiarity, inclusiveness, and operational feasibility.&#x20;

The governance model, therefore, evolves incrementally, with MVP1 establishing the structural foundations and MVP2 representing the intended operational model once the data space becomes fully functional.&#x20;

This evolutionary approach ensures that governance remains:&#x20;

## Overall outlook

The GDDS is envisioned to operate according to a [federated ](#user-content-fn-1)[^1]governance model; decision-making responsibilities are distributed across its governance bodies according to their respective mandates. This approach seeks to balance operational efficiency with appropriate governance oversight, ensuring that decisions can be taken promptly while maintaining transparency, accountability, and trust across the data space.

A key principle of the GDDS governance model, both in MVP1 and MVP2, is subsidiarity: decisions should be taken at the lowest governance level capable of addressing the matter effectively. Routine operational decisions are therefore handled through established operational processes, domain-specific matters are coordinated within the relevant Data Sharing Groups through their Orchestrators (in MVP2), and matters with broader governance implications are subject to additional review and supervision.

The governance structure has been designed to avoid unnecessary administrative complexity and to enable agile operation of the data space. Participants should be able to interact with the GDDS through clear and predictable governance processes without being subject to excessive procedural burdens. At the same time, appropriate mechanisms must exist to ensure consistency, fairness, and accountability in the application of the Rulebook and other governance instruments.

Decision-making within the GDDS therefore should follow a layered governance approach. As a result, it is proposed that in MVP2 the GDDS Operator is responsible for the execution of day-to-day operational activities, while the Data Space Governance Authority (DSGA) carries out executive governance functions, including the management of admission and compliance processes, within the framework set by the Council of Participants. The Supervisory Board provides oversight of governance implementation and reviews matters requiring additional supervision. The Council of Participants is the primary governance authority and provides direction and is responsible for decisions that affect the data space, its governance framework, or its long-term development, including approval and amendment of the Rulebook. This is described in more detail later on in the Section of [Governance Model (MVP2) section. ](governance-model-for-the-operational-gdds-mvp2.md)

Together, these governance bodies ensure that the GDDS can remain both operationally agile and strategically coherent, supporting the continuous growth and evolution of the data space while preserving the autonomy of Data Sharing Groups and their participants. The mechanism by which Data Sharing Groups set their own rules within the overarching GDDS Governance framework, including where a baseline rule may be relaxed, is described in [Domain-Level Governance.](governance-model-for-the-operational-gdds-mvp2.md#domain-level-governance-subsidiarity-principle)

## Transition Logic (MVP1 → MVP2)&#x20;

| Community of Practice         | Community of Practice                                  |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| General Assembly              | Council of Participants                                |
| Operations Team               | GDDS Operator                                          |
| Use Case Groups               | Data Sharing Groups                                    |
| Ethics Advisor                | Ethics & Compliance Committee                          |
| Strategic Steering Committee  | May evolve into the Supervisory Board or be dissolved  |

[^1]: For now: 'An arrangement in which multiple autonomous parties (for example, operators, registries, or identity providers) retain independence and control over their own resources while agreeing to interoperate under a common set of rules and a shared trust framework. Federation combines local autonomy with ecosystem-level coordination, as distinct from a centralised model (a single controlling authority) or an uncoordinated decentralised one (no shared rules).’
