# Contractual Frameworks

{% hint style="warning" %}

Since the project runs till 2028, the final GDDS deliverable is expected to have additional information on these sections._
{% endhint %}

Within the Green Deal Data Space (GDDS), the contractual framework serves as the foundation for establishing trust and ensuring clarity regarding the conditions under which participants join, operate and interact through the data space. It translates the governance principles and legal requirements of the GDDS into practical agreements that define roles, rights, and obligations. By providing common contractual terms, conditions, and templates, the framework helps reduce ambiguity, lowers transaction and negotiation costs, and facilitates participants’ onboarding.

Importantly, the contractual framework also provides the legal link between actions performed within the digital environment of the GDDS and their corresponding legal consequences in the real world. Through contractual commitments, actions conducted by participants using data space services can be attributed to legal entities and assessed against agreed rights and obligations. This enables governance, operational enforcement, dispute resolution, and, where necessary, the application of contractual remedies.

Building on the European Commission’s Model Contractual Terms (MCTs) for data access and use and the contractual architecture proposed by the Data Spaces Support Centre (DSSC), the GDDS contractual framework is structured around two complementary contractual layers. While the Participation Agreement establishes baseline obligations applicable to all participants, transaction-specific arrangements are defined separately. Where personal data processing is involved, additional GDPR-specific arrangements may be required depending on the roles and responsibilities of the parties in a particular transaction.

#### Participation Agreement (PA)

The Participation Agreement is the overarching governance agreement between GDDS and each participant. It establishes the legal basis for participation in the data space and binds all members to the GDDS Rulebook, including technical requirements, security obligations, and participation conditions.

The PA is designed as a role-neutral framework agreement, applying irrespective of whether a participant acts as a Data Rights Holder, Data Provider, Data User, Service Provider, or in multiple roles simultaneously.

The PA governs admission, onboarding, access profiles, compliance requirements, participation fees, data protection obligations, intellectual property principles, suspension and termination procedures, and dispute resolution mechanisms.

Importantly, the PA does not itself grant access to specific datasets nor create obligations to share data. Rather, it establishes the common governance conditions under which future data transactions may occur while preserving participants’ data sovereignty, understood in the GDDS context as the ability of participants to determine and control the conditions under which their data may be accessed, used, shared and re-used within the data space.

#### Transaction Level Agreement (TLA)

The Transaction Level Agreement is a bilateral (or potentially multilateral) agreement concluded between participants for a specific data-sharing transaction. It operates within the framework established by the Participation Agreement and the GDDS Rulebook.

The TLA is intended to provide a common contractual structure for data-sharing transactions through a set of standard contractual provisions, supplemented by transaction-specific terms. It specifies the Data Product or service concerned, access modalities, authorised uses, duration of access, technical requirements, confidentiality obligations, intellectual property provisions, liability allocation, fees, service conditions, and any transaction-specific GDPR arrangements that may be required.

Through Annex A, the parties define the precise scope of the authorised use, including access type, usage constraints, duration, technical specifications, and any applicable license conditions. The annex may also reference policy templates, usage policies, and standard conditions maintained within the GDDS framework.

The TLA therefore serves as the contractual instrument through which a participant exercises its data sovereignty by determining the conditions under which access to data or services is granted, and through which corresponding rights and obligations are conferred upon other participants.

To support interoperability and future automation, certain contractual conditions defined within the TLA may also be represented in machine-readable form and linked to technical policy enforcement mechanisms. Such machine-readable representations are intended to facilitate automated authorisation, access control, and policy enforcement within the GDDS. However, in the event of any inconsistency, the legally binding contractual terms agreed by the parties remain authoritative unless otherwise expressly specified.

{% hint style="warning" %}
_Note: Subject to further discussion within the consortium, Annex A of the Transaction Level Agreement (TLA), which defines the authorised use of the Data Product, including access profiles, access types, permitted uses and purposes, and licensing conditions, could be aligned with, or partially based on, the iSHARE Trust Framework Terms of Use (Conditions of Exchange). This could provide a standardised and interoperable framework for governing transaction-specific rights, obligations, access authorisations, and usage conditions between Data Holders and Data Users._&#x20;

_At the same time, caution should be exercised regarding the direct incorporation by reference of the iSHARE Terms of Use into Annex A. While the iSHARE framework offers a valuable reference model, its Terms of Use may be amended independently over time and outside the governance processes of the GDDS. Direct incorporation could therefore result in contractual provisions being modified without the explicit agreement of the parties to the TLA. From a legal certainty, it may be preferable either to incorporate and adapt the relevant iSHARE concepts directly into the GDDS contractual framework or, if the iSHARE Terms of Use are referenced, to ensure that all participants are promptly and adequately informed of any amendments and that appropriate mechanisms are in place to manage the contractual effects of such changes._
{% endhint %}
