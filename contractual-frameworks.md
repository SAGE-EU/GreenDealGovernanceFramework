# Contractual Frameworks

{% hint style="warning" %}

Since the project runs till 2028, the final GDDS deliverable is expected to have additional information on these sections._
{% endhint %}

Within the Green Deal Data Space (GDDS), the contractual framework serves as the foundation for establishing trust and ensuring clarity in how participants engage with the data space and interact with one another. It operationalises the governance framework by translating legal requirements, participation conditions, and data-sharing principles into binding contractual commitments. By providing standardised agreements and clearly allocated rights and obligations, the contractual framework reduces legal uncertainty, facilitates onboarding, and promotes consistent participation across the ecosystem.

Building on the European Commission’s Model Contractual Terms (MCTs) for data access and use and the contractual architecture proposed by the Data Spaces Support Centre (DSSC), the GDDS contractual framework is structured around two complementary contractual layers:

1. **Participation Agreement (PA):** The Participation Agreement is the overarching governance agreement between GDDS and each participant. It establishes the legal basis for participation in the data space and binds all members to the GDDS Rulebook, including technical requirements, security obligations, and participation conditions. The PA is role-neutral and applies irrespective of whether a participant acts as a Data Rights Holder, Data User/Consumer, Service Provider, or in multiple roles simultaneously. It governs admission, onboarding, access profiles, compliance requirements, participation fees, data protection obligations, intellectual property principles, suspension and termination procedures, and dispute resolution mechanisms. Importantly, the PA does not itself grant access to specific datasets nor create obligations to share data. Rather, it establishes the common governance conditions under which future data transactions may occur while preserving participants’ data sovereignty.
2. **Transaction Level Agreement (TLA):** The Transaction Level Agreement is a bilateral (or potentially multilateral) agreement concluded between participants for a specific data-sharing transaction. It operates within the framework established by the Participation Agreement and the GDDS Rulebook. The TLA specifies the particular Data Product being shared, the access modality, duration of access, usage rights, technical requirements, fees, confidentiality obligations, intellectual property provisions, liability allocation, and transaction-specific GDPR arrangements where applicable. Through Annex A, the parties define the precise scope of the authorised use, including access type, usage constraints, duration, technical specifications, and any applicable licence conditions. The TLA therefore serves as the contractual instrument through which data sovereignty is exercised by the Data Holder and specific rights are granted to the Data User.

{% hint style="warning" %}
_Note: Subject to further discussion within the consortium, Annex A of the Transaction Level Agreement (TLA), which defines the authorised use of the Data Product, including access profiles, access types, permitted uses and purposes, and licensing conditions, could be aligned with, or partially based on, the iSHARE Trust Framework Terms of Use (Conditions of Exchange). This could provide a standardised and interoperable framework for governing transaction-specific rights, obligations, access authorisations, and usage conditions between Data Holders and Data Users._&#x20;

_At the same time, caution should be exercised regarding the direct incorporation by reference of the iSHARE Terms of Use into Annex A. While the iSHARE framework offers a valuable reference model, its Terms of Use may be amended independently over time and outside the governance processes of the GDDS. Direct incorporation could therefore result in contractual provisions being modified without the explicit agreement of the parties to the TLA. From a legal certainty, it may be preferable either to incorporate and adapt the relevant iSHARE concepts directly into the GDDS contractual framework or, if the iSHARE Terms of Use are referenced, to ensure that all participants are promptly and adequately informed of any amendments and that appropriate mechanisms are in place to manage the contractual effects of such changes._
{% endhint %}
